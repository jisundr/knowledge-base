# Demand Factory

The counterpart to a software factory: agents that generate and understand market demand so that code output is rate-matched to what customers want. Without it, an efficient factory produces a **glut of software**: more screens and buttons than customers want or than change management can absorb. Customers want more gross margin, more customers, and brand differentiation, not more UI.

The idea is speculative: the source speaker says it is new and needs discovery itself, and calls building it the next job for engineers rather than something to leave to sales and marketing. A brownfield factory keeps existing promises; it cannot build the moat.

## Mechanisms

- **Prioritized market inquiry**: agents ask the market targeted questions.
- **Continuous synthesis**: agents process responses, tickets, and feedback overnight and re-rank priorities.
- **Automated experimentation**: agents schedule pricing-elasticity experiments within limited experiment bandwidth.
- **Latency reduction**: faster responses to customer requests invite prospects to state broader needs.

## Brand envelope

The set of adjacent products customers will accept from a brand. A sparkling-water brand moving into energy drinks stays inside it; selling motor oil breaks it. A demand factory finds adjacent opportunities and adds product lines inside the envelope.

## Growth ladder

Flow from the demand factory must not overwhelm customers' appetite for change, so growth comes from more customers and more share of wallet: more SKUs, grouped into product lines, then acquiring competitors. Competitors have the same models, but starting first still gives an edge.
